# Making the case

## What the data needs to show

1. **Volume** — total hours beyond (and between) visits.
2. **Invisibility** — how much was not patient-facing, so it never appears in a schedule.
3. **Misallocation** — how much did not require a physician, split into clerical vs care coordination.
4. **Displacement** — how much was non-complex healthy urgent work taking time from complex patients.
5. **Spillover** — after-hours work and interruptions, if you log them.

Two to four ordinary weeks is usually enough. Do not pick your worst week — an ordinary week is more persuasive and harder to dismiss.

## What not to say

- Do not frame it as "I am slow / behind / inefficient." Speed is not the variable the data measures.
- Do not frame it as a complaint about colleagues. Frame it as a staffing design question for a complex clinic.

## A short script

> I tracked all of my work for [period], patient-facing and not, with a one-click timer. I logged [X] hours in total. [Y] hours — [Z]% — did not require a physician. They were necessary for a complex care clinic: [top 2–3 delegable categories]. [A] hours were clerical and [B] hours were care coordination. [C] hours were non-complex healthy urgent work on top of complex care.
>
> That is roughly [Y ÷ weeks] hours a week of support work currently done by a physician — about [fraction] of a support FTE, at physician cost and using the scarcest clinic resource. I am asking for clerical and care-coordination support so that work is done by the right role and those hours go back to complex patients.

## Bring

- The 1-page summary (`sample-report.md`, filled in)
- The CSV export as backup, not as the opening move
- 3–5 concrete, generic examples (no PHI): e.g. "One DME follow-up took 4 calls, 38 minutes, none of which needed a doctor"
